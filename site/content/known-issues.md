SUMMARY: These are the reasons the numbers above are narrower than they look, and they should be read before the results, not after.

This section is not a disclaimer. These are the reasons the numbers above are
narrower than they look, and anyone reading the results should read these first.

- **The sample is small.** The run on this page is a pipeline calibration, not a
  benchmark. It is large enough to show that the measurement works and far too
  small to support a claim about any model's accuracy. Per-cell rates are
  withheld wherever the count is below the threshold, and where they are shown
  the raw counts are shown beside them.

- **Word error rate as computed here is not a meaningful accuracy figure.** The
  reference and the transcript may write the same number differently — one in
  digits, one spelled out — and every number word then counts as an error. This
  is discussed in the results section, where it is treated as a finding about the
  metric rather than as a defect to be apologised for.

- **Synthetic speech is not human speech.** The audio is generated by the same
  vendor whose recogniser is being measured. A model may well handle its own
  synthesiser's output better than it handles a real caller on a real handset,
  which would make these figures optimistic. Real-corpus runs against GramVaani,
  IndicVoices and Shrutilipi are the obvious control and have not been done.

- **One speaker, one language.** Every utterance uses a single TTS voice in
  Hindi. Speaker variation, accent variation and the other twenty-one languages
  are all unmeasured.

- **Person and place names are not scored.** They need an NER model, which is not
  wired in. Reporting zero accuracy for an extractor that does not exist would
  read as a finding, so those types are excluded rather than reported as failing.

- **Most declared conditions have not been run.** The conditions section
  describes all nine because the method is the point, but anything without data
  is marked as unmeasured and should not be read as coverage.
